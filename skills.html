SKILLS

AI Fluency Skills 1–10

Ten practical skills that map to the current principles. Read them as moves you can make in the next chat session.

1

SKILL 1

Learn Basic AI Skills

Use the copy button instead of dragging your mouse over the AI's response. Drag a screenshot into the chat and ask AI to explain what you see. Upload a file (Word document, PDF, image) into the chat so AI can review it. Paste a web link into the chat and ask AI to read what's at that link. Share a chat with a colleague using a share link. Ask AI to improve a prompt you drafted, then paste the improved version back into the same chat. Insert the same prompt into two different AI platforms (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) and compare answers.

2

SKILL 2

Ask AI How to Use AI

Open a chat and ask AI to walk you through how to set up a project, configure persistent memory, connect AI to your email or calendar, or build a reusable skill. Ask AI to find a YouTube video, an official documentation page, a Substack newsletter, or a podcast episode that explains a feature you want to learn. Drag a screenshot of an icon, a settings menu, or a sidebar into the chat and ask AI to explain what it is. Ask AI to explain abstract concepts like tokens, embeddings, RAG, agents, or context windows in plain language.

3

SKILL 3

Treat AI Like a Working Partner

Instead of drafting one long prompt and hitting submit, have a back-and-forth conversation with AI — exchange short messages, react to what AI says, adjust as you go. When AI gives you something useful, tell AI specifically what worked and ask AI to store the underlying principle in its memory. When AI goes off-track, tell AI specifically what went wrong and ask AI to update its memory so the same issue does not recur next time.

4

SKILL 4

Take It One Step at a Time

Write to AI: "Let's go step by step. Please give me only the first step." Then say: "Take it slowly. One thing at a time." If a step does not make sense, write: "Why are we doing this step?" or "This step doesn't make sense to me." When you get stuck mid-task, paste a screenshot and write: "Here's where I am — what do you think?" If a step did not work, write: "Let's back up a step." When you are ready to move on, write: "Looks good. Next step."

5

SKILL 5

Save and Reuse Productive Sessions

At the end of a useful chat, ask AI: "Do you need to store any memories from this chat to help us in the future?" Return to a dedicated chat when you are doing a recurring task. Create a project on your AI platform and load it with permanent instructions about how you want AI to behave. Ask AI to summarize the key points of a session (not the output — the actual session), download the summary, and upload it at the start of your next chat as a hand-off. Build a reusable AI skill that automates a workflow you do over and over.

6

SKILL 6

Design the Workflow Before Reviewing Documents

Before you point AI at a body of documents — a stack of contracts, a set of deposition transcripts, a due diligence binder — first define the analytical objective. What types of documents would blow up the case? What types of admissions would matter? What themes matter? What would be easy to miss? Tell AI specifically what to flag rather than merely summarize. Without this step, AI drifts toward the generic and you miss what matters.

7

SKILL 7

Check AI's Work for Accuracy

AI hallucinates. Ask AI to provide a numbered appendix of sources with workable links at the end of every response. Ask AI to include verbatim quotes from each source so you can stress-test the analysis. For documents you uploaded, ask AI to give you a page or paragraph reference for each claim in its summary. Stay alert for AI making up a source, conflating two different cases, getting math wrong, or stating something inaccurately. Do not trust. Verify.

8

SKILL 8

Lay a Foundation Before the Big Question

Borrowed from trial advocacy: establish the foundation with the witness before you ask what the witness knows. With AI, this means a sequence. First, tell AI the subject and confirm AI's understanding. Then tell AI the specific topic of the subject and confirm AI's understanding. Then ask AI to research the top five authoritative sources on that topic and report them back to you. Then ask AI to explain the topic at a high level using quotes and links from those sources. Only then do you ask your substantive question — and instruct AI to base its answer solely on the sources you just established.

9

Current Principle — Abstraction

Start at the Big Picture Before Diving Into Details

Tell AI to begin every response at the highest level of abstraction and wait for you to request more detail. Ask AI for multiple high-level options at one sentence each — for example: "Please give me five approaches to this task. Just one sentence for each." Make sure you understand the topic at the high level before asking AI to go deeper. When you notice you are deep in details you do not yet need, write: "Let's circle back to a higher level."

10

SKILL 10

Human Judgment Still Drives AI Success

AI amplifies expertise; it does not replace it. The professionals best positioned to leverage AI are the ones with the most experience — those who already bring judgment, reasoning, reading ability, listening skill, the instinct to ask the right question, and a sense of what actually matters. AI Fluency unlocks that advantage. Without those human capabilities, AI just produces faster generic output.
