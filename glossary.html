APPENDIX 1

Glossary — AI Terms

Brief plain-language explanations of concepts that come up in AI Fluency work.

ACEGLMNPRSTV
Agent
An AI system that doesn't just answer questions but takes actions — creating calendar invites, sending emails, browsing the web, running code, completing multi-step tasks. A chatbot responds; an agent does.
AI Skill
A reusable instruction set you build or install on your AI platform to teach the AI how to perform a specific kind of task — drafting an NDA, running a deposition prep checklist, generating a weekly status report in your preferred format. Skills work like a saved playbook the AI can run on demand, often triggered by name. Different platforms use different names for the same idea — Skills on Claude, Custom GPTs on ChatGPT, Gems on Gemini.
AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning
Three related but distinct terms. AI is the broad field of building machines that perform tasks we associate with human intelligence. Machine Learning is a subset of AI in which systems learn patterns from data rather than from explicit programming. Deep Learning is a subset of Machine Learning that uses large, layered neural networks — and is the engine behind today's most powerful AI tools.
Context Window
The amount of text an AI can hold in active "memory" at one time during a single chat session. Anything beyond the context window starts to fall away — which is why very long chats can begin to drift or lose track of earlier details.
Embedding
A vector that represents the meaning of a specific piece of content — a word, sentence, paragraph, or image. "Embedding" is the act of converting that content into the numerical form an AI can compare.
Generative AI
AI that creates new content — text, images, code, audio, video — rather than only classifying or analyzing existing content. ChatGPT, Claude, and image generators like Midjourney are all generative AI.
LLM (Large Language Model)
A type of AI system trained on enormous amounts of text to predict and generate language. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, and Grok are all LLMs.
MCP (Model Context Protocol)
An open standard that lets AI systems plug into outside tools and data sources in a consistent way — so an AI can connect to your email, calendar, files, or company systems without each platform building a one-off integration. Think of it as the universal adapter for connecting AI to the rest of your software.
Multimodal AI
AI that can work with more than one kind of input or output at the same time — text, images, audio, video. When you drag a screenshot into a chat and ask the AI to explain what it shows, you are using a multimodal capability.
Neural Network
The underlying architecture of modern AI — a system of mathematical layers loosely inspired by the way neurons in the human brain pass signals to one another. Most modern AI tools are built on neural networks.
Persistent Memory vs. Session-Only Context
Two different ways AI holds onto what you have told it. Session-only context lives just for the current chat — close the chat and it is gone. Persistent memory carries across chats: facts the AI remembers about you, your preferences, ongoing projects, the way you like to work. Persistent memory is what makes AI feel like it knows you over time rather than meeting you fresh each session.
Projects
A workspace on an AI platform that holds permanent instructions, uploaded files, and chat history for a recurring topic or workstream. Within a project, the AI carries forward what it knows about your work — your preferred style, the documents you have shared, your goals — across multiple sessions, rather than starting fresh each time. Claude Projects and ChatGPT Projects are the clearest examples.
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
A technique that lets an AI pull information from outside sources (your documents, a database, the web) before answering, rather than relying only on what it learned during training. RAG is how AI can answer questions about your firm's specific files without having been trained on them.
Semantic Search
Searching by meaning rather than by exact words. Traditional keyword search looks for the literal word "car." Semantic search would also surface results about "vehicle," "automobile," or "sedan" because they mean similar things.
System Prompt vs. User Prompt
Two kinds of instructions an AI receives. The system prompt is set by the platform or by you ahead of time and shapes how the AI behaves throughout a session — its tone, role, what it will and will not do. The user prompt is what you type into the chat box. System prompts are usually invisible to you in the moment but powerfully influence every response. Custom GPTs, projects, and saved instructions are all ways of setting system prompts.
Tokens
The basic units an AI reads and produces — roughly chunks of words or word-fragments rather than whole sentences. AI platforms typically measure both your input and the AI's output in tokens, and pricing and context-window limits are usually expressed in tokens.
Vector Database
A specialized database designed to store embeddings and let an AI quickly find which stored content is most similar in meaning to a new query. Vector databases are the storage layer underneath most RAG systems.
Vectors
A mathematical way of representing meaning as a long list of numbers. AI converts words, sentences, and even images into vectors so it can compare meanings — words with similar meanings end up with similar vectors.
